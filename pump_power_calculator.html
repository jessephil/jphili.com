Governing equations

Phyd = ΔP · Q Pin = Phyd / η = ΔP · Q / η hp = (gpm · psi) / (1714 · η)
Pin = shaft power required Phyd = hydraulic power delivered ΔP = discharge − suction pressure Q = volumetric flow rate η = overall efficiency (0–1)

Result is mechanical power at the pump shaft. To size the electric motor, divide by motor efficiency as well (typically 90–96% for industrial motors), and check that the motor can cover peak pressure and flow. Overall efficiency is the product of volumetric and mechanical (hydraulic) efficiency and varies with pressure, speed, viscosity, and wear — use the manufacturer’s performance curve at your actual operating point when you have one. The efficiency ranges in the dropdown are general industry guidance for new pumps at or near rated conditions, not tabulated standards; centrifugal efficiency in particular depends strongly on size and distance from the best-efficiency point. The lost power is dissipated mainly as heat in the fluid, which matters for reservoir and cooler sizing.
